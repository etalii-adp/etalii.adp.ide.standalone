THE RETRY, FOUND BY DEVELOPER 5, and it corrects a claim this lifecycle first made. Keep-last-good was justified by "the watcher's next event re-reads the finished file". That is false when the refusal lands on a write's LAST event: nothing follows, so the change is lost for good rather than late. Developer 5 traced the EditorResolution 60-second flake to that mechanism in c4's store. Landed without a retry, this lifecycle would have spread it from two stores to all ten.
- A refused read (`IOException` or `UnauthorizedAccessException`) is now retried 5 times, 50 ms apart, before anything is concluded.
- On a reload, a missing body (including `FileNotFoundException` or `DirectoryNotFoundException` after the existence check) is retried in the same loop. A missing body on a FIRST open is not retried, because it is a new document.
- The window is a choice rather than a measurement, sized wider than `PlainEditorSession`'s 3 x 20 ms because a sharing violation after a save may be held longer than a file missing mid-replace. What held the file is unknown, so a read that needed a retry logs `Read {Path} on attempt {Attempt} of {Attempts}`, and those lines are the instrument for the real hold.
- RETRYING NARROWS THE WINDOW AND DOES NOT CLOSE IT: a hold longer than about a fifth of a second still loses the change on the last event. A delayed re-read after keep-last-good would close it, and is deferred until the attempt-count lines show reads needing all five tries.
- THIS DOES NOT FIX THE FLAKE ON DEVELOP, whose own c4 and causal-loop stores have no retry until task 6 converts them. Developer 5 is mirroring these exact semantics store-locally on `claude/refused-reload-retry`, so task 6 moves the code without changing its behaviour.
