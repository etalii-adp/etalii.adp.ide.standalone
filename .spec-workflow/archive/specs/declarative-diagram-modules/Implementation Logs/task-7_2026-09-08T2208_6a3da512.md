THE RULER, moved here by Architect 2 after reading its mechanism rather than its purpose. A view-fixed chrome kind needs something background does not have: entries DERIVED from the visible range rather than a fixed list.

The split that makes it declarable at all: THE LADDER IS DATA, CHOOSING A RUNG AND WALKING IT IS MECHANISM. A module states its rungs - a fixed step in its own unit, or a named calendar step - with a label format per rung; the library picks the coarsest that fits and walks it. The alternative is a module supplying a function, which is the escape hatch this specification exists to close. Calendar rungs are named rather than approximated because a month is not 2,629,746 seconds and a reader expects a label on the 1st.

TWO OF MY OWN TESTS WERE VACUOUS AND BOTH ARE NOW FIXED. This is the part worth recording, because both passed against the defect and both looked right.

The first was arithmetic: I expected six year labels across five years and got five, because 5x365 days crosses the 2028 leap year and lands short of 1 Jan 2031. An exact list caught my own error where a count would have passed silently.

The second is the real one, and it is the "invisible to whoever wrote it" case exactly. The UTC guard used a DATE probe at midnight UTC, which shifts only under a NEGATIVE offset - this runner is UTC+1 in January, so 1 Jan stays 1 Jan and local-time formatting PASSED. I only saw it fail by forcing TZ=America/Los_Angeles, which is a guard that works nowhere it actually runs. Replaced with an hour label, which moves under every non-zero offset.

AND THAT SECOND VERSION WAS ALSO VACUOUS, for a different reason worth naming: toContain("23:00") still passed against the defect, because local time shifts every label by the SAME amount and the 22:00 tick simply becomes 23:00. A uniform shift is invisible to a membership check by construction. Only an exact list discriminates - and it does: the sabotage now reddens on this machine with no TZ forced.

The remaining limit is stated in the test rather than hidden: on a runner genuinely at UTC nothing can tell the two apart, because there is no difference to observe.
