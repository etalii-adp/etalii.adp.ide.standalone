# Source of this example

This example redraws "Diagram 7: CLD telling the story of what impacts cod population in the UK" from:

> Kamath, A., Palmer, F. I., Manikarachchige, I. and Hutton, C. (2024). *Causal Loop Diagram*. National Centre for Research Methods online learning resource. University of Southampton. Available at https://repository.ncrm.ac.uk/resources/online/all/causal_loop-diagram (accessed 31 October 2026).

The figure is the last of seven on that page. Diagrams 3 to 6 on the same page are its four parts (environmental, social, economic and regulatory aspects), and the resource's two videos build it step by step; their transcripts (`Transcript-Causal_Loop_Diagram1.docx` and `Transcript-Causal_Loop_Diagram2.docx`, downloadable from the page) were used to read the handwriting and the direction of arrows where the figure itself is unclear.

## Reading the figure

The figure is handwritten. Everything the example states that the figure does not state plainly is marked in `uk-cod-population.cld`:

- A variable the figure writes twice, once in grey as a reminder (consumer demand, the hospitality sector and the recruitment rate), is one variable.
- The link from the juvenile mortality rate to the juvenile cod population is not drawn; the first video's transcript states it ("as mortality rate increases, the cod population decreases"), and the figure's R beside it needs it.
- The arrow into legal fishing grounds leaves the fishing mortality rate rather than the adult cod population, as drawn.
- Three arrows the figure leaves without a polarity carry the one they plainly mean (habitat to spawning, and the double arrow between world and local price); the others stay without one, carrying capacity's included.
- Three of the figure's twelve R and B marks cannot be tied to one cycle and are not assigned; four of the nine that can be disagree with the arithmetic of their arrows, and keep the figure's letter.
